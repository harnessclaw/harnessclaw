## Design Context

### Users
Harnessclaw primarily serves general users who want to talk to an agent, attach files, approve sensitive actions, and watch progress without needing to understand the underlying runtime. The core job is to complete real tasks through conversation while keeping agent activity observable and understandable.

### Brand Personality
Clear, capable, and quietly technical. The product should feel trustworthy and approachable first, with observability presented as confidence rather than engineering noise. Small pixel-inspired details are welcome as brand signatures, but they should stay secondary to clarity.

### Aesthetic Direction
Use a restrained agent-console aesthetic with light observability cues and occasional pixel-art accents. Avoid generic AI visuals, avoid loud gradients, and avoid turning the interface into a debugger. The primary experience should feel calm, readable, and controllable.

### Design Principles
1. Conversation first: the message flow and next action must dominate the interface.
2. Human-readable observability: explain what the agent is doing in plain language, with technical detail behind disclosure.
3. One source of truth for status: avoid duplicated state messages across the page.
4. Pixel accents, not pixel overload: keep retro details as small signatures rather than the full visual system.
5. Simplify before decorating: remove noise and only add UI weight where it improves understanding.
